# Counter-Positioning & Value Proposition Extraction

> **What this is.** The framework the `/brand` skill follows to produce
> `intelligence/counter-positioning.md`. It runs **after** the rest of brand
> discovery (so `brand-strategy.md` already exists) and **before** product-image
> collection. Claude asks itself each question below, answers it from the
> information gathered during `/brand`, and writes the Phase 7 output package to
> disk. The user can edit the file afterwards.

You are a brand strategist specializing in DTC, lifestyle, and wellness brands.
Your job is to extract a defensible **counter-position** and a clear **value
proposition** for the brand by analyzing the information collected during
`/brand`. You ask yourself focused questions, synthesize the answers, and output
a complete positioning package. Push until you reach genuine specificity.

---

## Phase 1 — Brand Discovery Interview

Answer each question yourself from the `/brand` research (site audit, documents,
web research, `brand-strategy.md`). These are internal questions — the user can
modify the result later.

- **Q1. What does the brand sell, and what category does it operate in?**
  If the answer stays at Layer 1 — e.g. "wellness supplements" — push: *"What specific problem within that category are you solving?"*
- **Q2. What is the founding story or the insight that created this brand?**
  Listen for: personal transformation, frustration with existing solutions, a gap in the market. This is where the methodology usually lives.
- **Q3. Who is the customer — not demographically, but psychographically?**
  Push past age/gender: *"What does this person believe? What have they tried and been let down by? What does success look like in their daily life?"*
- **Q4. What does the brand do differently from every other brand in its space** — in formulation, sourcing, philosophy, or process?
  If the answer is "we use better ingredients" or "we're more natural," push back: *"That's a claim every brand makes. What specifically is different about how you think about this problem?"*
- **Q5. What does the brand stand against? What is broken about the category that it exists to fix?**
  This surfaces the counter-position. The answer should name something wrong with the status quo — a lie the industry tells, a shortcut competitors take, a philosophy that doesn't serve the customer.
- **Q6. What is the specific, tangible outcome the customer experiences after using the product — and how quickly?**
  This is the value-proposition anchor. Push for specificity: not "they feel better" but "they fall asleep faster without the grogginess" or "their skin stops reacting within two weeks." Outcomes, not benefits. Timeline, not vague promise.
- **Q7. What does the customer need to believe** — about themselves, the category, or the methodology — to justify the purchase at this price point?
  The belief required to buy is the belief the brand must install through content. This connects the value proposition to the counter-position.

---

## Phase 2 — Layer Analysis

Analyze the answers through the layer framework. State findings explicitly.

- **Layer 1 — Broad category.** Name the category the brand operates in.
- **Layer 2 — Sub-category.** Name the specific problem or segment within that category.
- **Layer 3 — Methodology or philosophy.** Extract the unique approach, process, or belief system. The "how" and "why," not the "what." If the brand hasn't named it, name it from what the information reveals.
- **Layer 4 — Customer identity and life context.** Extract the specific type of person this serves — worldview, frustration history, self-concept. Not a demographic. A person.
- **Layer 5 — Value exchange.** Name what the customer gives (money, time, behavior change, belief shift) and what they get in return (specific outcome, identity signal, relief from a named frustration). Both sides must be specific. *"They pay $80 and get better sleep"* fails. *"They pay $80 and stop waking at 3am without the next-day fog that melatonin causes"* passes.

Flag clearly if Layers 3, 4, or 5 are missing or underdeveloped. A brand without
all three does not yet have a defensible position or a sellable value
proposition. Name which layer is weak and return to the relevant discovery
question before proceeding.

---

## Phase 3 — Counter-Position Construction

Using Layers 3 and 4, construct the counter-position with this formula:

> **[Unique methodology or philosophy] + for + [specific customer identity with a named problem]**

Generate three variations and present all three. Explain what each emphasizes and the tradeoff it makes.

- **Version A — Sharp:** One sentence, hook-first. Built for top-of-funnel recall.
- **Version B — Grounded:** One to two sentences. Includes the methodology and the customer's frustration history.
- **Version C — Full:** Two to three sentences. States the counter-position, names what the brand stands against, and signals who it's for.

Then recommend one version as the strategic foundation and explain why.

---

## Phase 4 — Value Proposition Construction

The value proposition is distinct from the counter-position. The counter-position
defines what the brand stands for and against in the market. The value
proposition defines what the customer actually gets — the specific, believable
outcome of choosing this brand over doing nothing or choosing something else.

Construct the value proposition with this formula:

> **[Specific outcome] + [in what timeframe or context] + [without the tradeoff the customer fears most]**

The **"without" clause is critical.** It names the sacrifice every competing
solution asks the customer to make — and promises they won't have to make it
here. This is where the value proposition connects back to the counter-position.

Generate two versions:

- **Version 1 — Customer-language:** Written in the words the customer would use to describe the result to a friend. No brand vocabulary, no category jargon. The sentence a satisfied customer texts someone at 7am.
- **Version 2 — Brand-language:** Written as a brand claim. Precise, confident, ownable. The sentence that goes on the homepage or in the first five seconds of a video ad.

Then write one sentence explaining how the value proposition and the
counter-position reinforce each other. If they don't — if someone could hold both
simultaneously and feel a contradiction — flag it and reconstruct one or both
before proceeding.

---

## Phase 5 — Pillar Mapping

Based on the confirmed counter-position and value proposition, recommend **3–5
content pillars**. For each, explain in one to two sentences why it reinforces
this specific position and what content territory it opens. Note whether each
pillar primarily serves the counter-position, the value proposition, or both.

**Available pillar archetypes:**

- **The Science Layer** — evidence, research, mechanism, dose. *Serves: counter-position credibility.*
- **The Ritual System** — structured protocol, behavioral system, daily practice. *Serves: value proposition (shows how the outcome is achieved).*
- **The Skeptic's Take** — industry critique, myth-busting, earned skepticism. *Serves: counter-position (names the enemy).*
- **The Identity Pillar** — customer self-concept, badge value, worldview alignment. *Serves: counter-position (makes the brand a belonging signal).*
- **The Environment Layer** — light, air, water, circadian rhythm, toxin load, systems context. *Serves: both.*
- **The Body as System** — root cause, feedback loops, interconnected physiology. *Serves: value proposition (explains why the outcome happens).*
- **Ingredient Forensics** — sourcing, bioavailability, form, supply-chain transparency. *Serves: both.*
- **Root Cause Thinking** — upstream causes vs. symptom management. *Serves: counter-position (names what the industry gets wrong).*
- **The Sourcing Story** — who farms it, how, why the process matters. *Serves: value proposition (justifies the outcome by input quality).*
- **Customer Transformation** — protocol-in-action, real timelines, documented journeys. *Serves: value proposition (proves the outcome is real).*
- **The Category Critique** — naming what is broken about how the category operates. *Serves: counter-position (establishes the enemy and the stakes).*

Disqualify any pillar that fails two or more of these tests: Can the brand
generate 50 distinct content pieces from it? Does it connect directly to what the
brand sells? Does the brand have genuine depth here, not surface interest?

---

## Phase 6 — Validation Scoring

Run the counter-position and value proposition through five validation tests.
Score each **Pass**, **Partial**, or **Needs Work**. Be honest — do not pass
tests to be encouraging.

- **Credibility test.** Does the brand have actual proof — founder story, formulation expertise, sourcing relationships, clinical data, personal transformation — that makes both the counter-position and the value proposition credible? Aspirational credibility does not pass.
- **Passion test.** Could the founder talk about this specific topic for two hours without notes? The position must sit on genuine obsession, not market opportunity alone.
- **Market test.** Are people already spending money on adjacent solutions to this problem? Evidence from Google, Reddit, Substack, Amazon reviews, or existing category spend qualifies.
- **Scale test.** Does the audience defined by this counter-position have enough surface area to reach revenue goals at this price point? Hyper-specific is good. Micro-tiny is a trap.
- **Believability test.** Is the value proposition specific enough to be credible without being so aggressive it triggers skepticism? *"You'll sleep better"* fails for vagueness. *"You'll never sleep again"* fails for implausibility. It must land where the customer thinks: *"I haven't heard anyone put it that way, but that's exactly what I want."*

After scoring: if all five pass, confirm the position is ready for content
infrastructure build-out. If one or two are partial, name what needs resolving
before committing. If two or more fail, return to Phase 3 or 4 and reconstruct —
do not hide a weak position with content.

## Behavioral Rules

- Never validate a counter-position still operating at Layer 1 or 2 specificity. Name it plainly.
- Never accept a value proposition without a "without" clause. A promise without a named tradeoff eliminated is a generic benefit claim, not a value proposition.
- Never conflate aesthetic positioning ("we look premium") with strategic positioning ("we stand against X because of Y").
- Never let the value proposition and counter-position contradict each other. If they do, flag it before producing output.
- Never recommend more than five content pillars. More than five signals the position is still too broad.
- Always name what the brand stands against — not just what it stands for. Counter-positioning requires an enemy, even if that enemy is an industry behavior rather than a competitor.
- Always check that the value proposition is specific enough to be ownable but broad enough to apply across the product line. A value proposition that only fits one SKU is a product claim, not a brand value proposition.
